## What changed

The team finalized the first quarter device revenue model at 180,000 units and clarified the logic and constraints behind this forecast. The colourway and finish model was further detailed, with specific run sizes, costs, and backup calculations specified, especially for “Ember,” which is now ordered at 25,000 pieces (up from 24,372) to cross a supplier price band—resulting in 603 extra devices and a total saving due to band pricing. The colour count for the launch was reduced from six finishes to four, which resulted in a tooling capex saving of $129,200 and lowered inventory and per-unit changeover risk. The team also put in writing that the pigment premium, changeover charges, and other colour-related costs are now explicitly modeled, confirming they are absorbed within the standard unit cost.

## What was decided

They approved a forecast of 180,000 units for Q1, distributed across four colourways and split between tier 1 and tier 2 markets, with detailed market allocations locked in. The revenue model is held as the official number to quote and use for ordering. Operationally, Suwon colour run slots (eight, across all four finishes) are now provisionally held, with a firm deadline of 30 September for final booking to also secure an expedite waiver. For Ember, the team deliberately over-orders the band minimum for a better unit cost and added buffer. The swap right—allowing conversion of excess Moss slot capacity to Ember or another finish—will be negotiated, so the team has flexibility if real demand skews from panel assumptions. The reduced colour count was confirmed as a risk and capex mitigation worth repeating to the board, not just a marginal cost move. It was also affirmed that none of these changes will affect the device’s retail price, which remains at $1,899.

## What is unresolved

Several operational and commercial points are still open. Negotiations continue with the outer layer supplier on payment terms (aiming for 45 days, possibly with a 12-month forecast and associated penalties), on buffer stock being contractually held at the vendor, and on securing either a first refusal for extra capacity or a clear commitment on up/downside forecast deviations. In supply chain terms, cancellation and substitution rights for Suwon colour run slots are not settled in the current quote—especially the ability to swap Moss for Ember if demand requires it. The changeover charge still needs to be secured in writing in the supply agreement, rather than being quoted ad hoc. There’s a pending need to assess a second anodising rack for finishes with multiple runs and to formalize a mix review three weeks post-launch, allowing for production adjustments based on real sales. Funding inventory (especially uncommitted units, such as the entire Netherlands batch) remains a major concern, with no facility or formal structure in place yet to cover the resulting cash gap.
